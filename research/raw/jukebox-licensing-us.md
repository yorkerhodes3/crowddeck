# jukebox-licensing-us

QUERY: commercial jukebox music licensing US ASCAP BMI SESAC jukebox license JLO Jukebox License Office

## Synthesized answer
The Jukebox License Office (JLO) was a joint‑venture clearinghouse created by the three major U.S. performing‑rights organizations—ASCAP, BMI and SESAC—that historically offered a single, economical annual license covering public performances of any song in all three repertories for qualifying coin‑operated jukeboxes (machines operated by patrons with coins or tokens, used solely for non‑dramatic music playback in venues that do not charge admission). To qualify, the jukebox must be a “coin‑operated phonorecord player” as defined by U.S. copyright law, and the license required the display of a certificate on the unit. While many operators previously obtained this unified license through the JLO, the JLO ceased operations in 2025; consequently, owners of commercial jukeboxes now must secure separate annual jukebox licenses directly from ASCAP, BMI and SESAC, each covering that organization’s catalog, and continue to ensure the jukebox meets the statutory criteria and displays any required proof of licensing.
