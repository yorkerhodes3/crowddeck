QUERY: restaurant bar background music licensing ASCAP BMI SESAC GMR rates venue

## Synthesized answer
Restaurant and bar venues must obtain separate blanket licenses from each of the four U.S. performance‑rights organizations—ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR—to legally play recorded or live background music, and the fees depend on size, seating capacity, square footage, number of sound zones, outdoor playback and whether live performances, DJs or karaoke are offered; ASCAP licenses typically start around $300‑$500 per year for small locations and can rise to $390‑$600 for larger spaces, BMI fees range from about $250 for a modest bar up to $2,000 for bigger restaurants, SESAC and GMR are invitation‑only and do not publish standard rates but venues usually negotiate premiums that add several hundred dollars each, resulting in a combined annual cost that often exceeds $1,000 per site—with one example pricing set at $375 per location for ASCAP, BMI and SESAC plus $143 for GMR (about $518 total), while bundled services such as SoundMachine charge roughly $26.95 a month (≈$323 / year) for all four PROs, and most venues should budget anywhere from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars annually based on these variables.

### A Significant Cost for Operators

Blanket licenses have become a significant cost for restaurateurs with ASCAP, BMI, GMR, and SESAC lining up for fees. The PROs use formulas based on occupancy, number of seats, whether the music played is recorded or live, played by a DJ, played for the purpose of karaoke, and even categories of live music, among other factors.
